Tire Care Tips for the 2020 Toyota Prius

  • Passenger cars lose small amounts of air every month without any visible sign. A quick monthly check with a reliable gauge keeps fuel economy, braking and tire wear on target.
  • Check pressure when the tires are cold: before driving, or after the vehicle has been parked for at least three hours.
  • Your tire pressure monitoring system (TPMS) typically warns only when pressure falls around 25% below the recommended value, so it does not replace a regular manual check.
  • Front and rear pressures differ on at least one version of this vehicle, so set each axle separately instead of using one value for all four tires.
  • This is a 6-year-old model year, so the tires on it have probably been replaced. If the new tires are a different size or load rating from the sizes above, confirm the correct pressure with a tire shop.

2020 Toyota Prius Tire Pressure FAQ

What is the recommended tire pressure for the 2020 Toyota Prius?

For every version listed, the recommended cold pressure is 36 PSI front (248 kPa) and 35 PSI rear (241 kPa).

What tire sizes does the 2020 Toyota Prius use?

The sizes listed for this model year are 195/65R15. The size is printed on the sidewall of your tire, so compare it with the table above to find your trim.

Should I inflate to the maximum pressure printed on the tire?

No. The number on the sidewall is the maximum the tire can safely hold, not the pressure recommended for your vehicle. Use the 35-36 PSI range from the table above or the placard on your door jamb instead.

How much does cold weather change my tire pressure?

As a rule of thumb, tire pressure drops by about 1 PSI for every 10°F (about 5.5°C) drop in temperature. A tire set to 36 PSI at around 68°F can read roughly 32-33 PSI after a night at 32°F, so check pressure more often in winter.

Is this pressure still correct if I replaced my tires?

If the replacement tires are the same size and load rating as the original ones, the placard pressure still applies. If you changed size or load rating, ask the tire shop to confirm the right pressure.

Why Tire Size Changes Recommended PSI

Car manufacturers test each vehicle configuration thoroughly. Larger diameter wheels with lower profile tires often require different internal air volumes compared to standard base-model wheels. Always match your exact tire sidewall dimensions to the specifications in the table above.

Remember to check your pressure when tires are cold (before driving or after the car has sat for 3+ hours). Temperature shifts also play a role: for every 10°F drop outside, your tire pressure drops by roughly 1 PSI.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Where can I find the official tire placard on my 2020 Toyota Prius?

The certification label is located on the driver’s side door pillar (jamb). It lists the official vehicle placard pressures for your exact factory setup.

Can I use the maximum PSI written on the tire sidewall?

Never use the max PSI printed on the tire rubber. That number is the absolute structural limit for the tire itself, not the vehicle manufacturer's safety recommendation.

Does towing or heavy payload require higher pressure?

Yes. If you are hauling heavy loads or towing with your Toyota Prius, check your owner’s manual, as rear tires may need additional PSI to support the weight safely.
